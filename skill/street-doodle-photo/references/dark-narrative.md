# Dark Narrative Mode

## Intent

Create an atmospheric underground editorial image in which fluorescent drawing seems to grow from the photograph's darkness, shadows, reflections, or object personalities. Favor integration, restraint, character, and close-view discoveries over blanket color coverage.

## Narrative Line System

- Preserve naturally dark photographic areas as visual breathing room. Allow restrained full-frame grading for integration, but do not crush the source into a uniformly black image or replace its photographic mood.
- On a bright source, build selective black or deep neutral scribble islands only where needed for contrast. Never place one opaque black wash over the entire photograph.
- Use turquoise as the main connective line network: smoke, electrical currents, creatures, aura paths, or hidden-world contours.
- Use yellow for expressive anatomy, faces, hands, feet, sparks, and small directional gestures.
- Use coral for one or two localized eruptions, impact bursts, pressure zones, or emotional punctuation behind or beside a subject.
- Let lines be dense in one or two narrative clusters and nearly absent in a deliberate calm zone.
- Keep large high-chroma flat masses optional and restrained, typically about 3–15% of the frame. A concentrated local burst is preferable to broad coverage.
- Make personification source-specific: separate bottles, machines, instruments, or objects should have distinct attitudes rather than repeated generic faces.

## Hierarchy

- One dominant atmospheric line ecosystem or character relationship.
- Two to four personified or surreal subject interactions.
- One localized coral or yellow eruption when compositionally useful.
- Five to twelve micro-marks, small eyes, notes, fists, sparks, or hidden figures.
- One large dimensional phrase woven into the darkness, smoke, architecture, or subject group.

## Structural Spine

- Choose one real rail, cable, shelf edge, reflection path, instrument contour, shadow boundary, or object group as the main connective spine.
- Let two or three secondary motifs branch from that spine so the drawing reads as one ecosystem rather than scattered decoration.
- Use one object cluster for the main character relationship and keep the face, hands, important labels, and logos outside the densest line work.

## Quality Test

- Fluorescent marks feel embedded in the original atmosphere rather than pasted on top.
- The first read is photographic mood plus one coherent illustrated ecosystem, not a collection of stickers.
- Turquoise line work carries the environment, yellow animates character, and coral remains locally forceful.
- Personified subjects communicate different roles or attitudes and form a small narrative.
- Dark or quiet areas remain spacious enough to make the glowing marks feel intentional.
- The result resembles an underground music poster, punk editorial illustration, or authored bar-wall drawing without resorting to brick-wall or tag clichés.
- Do not reject this mode merely because high-chroma flat masses fall below Bold Color's 18–40% requirement.
